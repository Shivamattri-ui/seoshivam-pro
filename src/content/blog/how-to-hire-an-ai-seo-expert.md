You searched for an AI SEO expert, and now your inbox is a wall of pitches that all sound the same. Twelve-month contracts, slick dashboards, a promise to get you cited in ChatGPT. Almost none of them can show you where they are cited themselves.

That gap is the whole problem. AI search rewired how buyers pick companies, and a wave of agencies rewired their pitch decks to match, without changing the work underneath. Hire the wrong one and you pay twice: once for the retainer, and again for the months you fall further behind while a competitor becomes the answer the AI hands your buyer.

Here is how to tell the real thing from the relabel: what the work actually is, what it should cost in 2026, and how to hire someone who moves your pipeline instead of your rankings chart.

## What an AI SEO expert actually does in 2026

Start here, because the title means less than it used to. "SEO expert" in 2019 meant keywords, backlinks, and a rankings dashboard. In 2026 that is half a job.

A real [AI SEO expert](/) works two surfaces at once: the ten blue links, and the AI answer sitting on top of them. When a buyer asks ChatGPT or Google's AI Overview which vendor to trust, they get one answer with one or two sources named. Getting your brand into that answer is [answer engine optimization](/services/aeo), and it runs on machinery most SEOs never touch: crawl access for AI bots, entity data a model can resolve, structured markup, and content written so a machine can lift a clean, quotable answer straight out of it.

None of that replaces classic SEO. It sits on the same foundation. The same content cluster that ranks on Google is what earns the citation in AI, which is why [how AEO differs from SEO](/insights/aeo-vs-seo-difference) is a difference of surface, not of fundamentals. Anyone who treats them as two separate budgets is selling you two retainers for one job.

So the real test of the role is simple. Can this person explain, in specifics, how a page becomes a citation? That is really a test of whether they understand [what AEO actually is](/insights/what-is-aeo) at the mechanical level. If the answer is "we create high-quality content," you are talking to a content marketer with a new business card.

## Your three options: freelancer, agency, or in-house

Before you vet a single person, decide what shape of help you are buying. There are three, and the right one depends on your stage, not on which has the best-looking deck.

A freelancer or independent consultant is one senior operator who does the work directly. You get judgment and speed, and the person on the sales call is the person touching your site. This fits most companies below enterprise scale, where the constraint is fixing the foundation and getting a handful of pages cited, not running production at volume.

An agency is a production line: account manager, strategist, technical lead, writers, a reporting layer. That machine earns its retainer when you have hundreds of pages across several markets and no time to touch any of it. Below that, you are paying for scale you cannot use yet, and your budget buys a thin slice of each role instead of a full pair of senior hands. The full breakdown of [consultant or an agency](/insights/aeo-consultant-vs-aeo-agency) is its own decision, and worth reading before you sign anything.

In-house is maximum control and maximum cost: $70,000 to $110,000 a year in the US for a capable hire, plus tooling, and you still have to know enough to manage them. It makes sense once AI search is core to your growth and you want the muscle inside the building.

Most companies get this wrong by buying agency scale for a consultant-sized problem. The honest tell is volume. If the bottleneck is "we do not publish enough," that points to an agency. If it is "we publish and still do not get found," that points to a specialist. An [AI SEO consultant](/ai-seo-consultant) fixes the second problem faster and cheaper than a staffed team will.

## The signals that separate a real expert from a relabel

The AI SEO market has no certification, no standard scope, and no agreed output checklist. That is why a content shop from 2018 can add "AEO and AI search" to a service page and sound credible to a buyer who does not know what to look for. Four signals separate them.

They can run a live citation check on the spot. Ask them to open ChatGPT and Perplexity on the call and show you where your brand appears for the queries your buyers actually type. Someone who works in citations daily does this in minutes. Someone who does not will steer you back to the case study deck.

They can explain why the engines are not interchangeable. Microsoft Copilot runs on Bing, so Bing indexation is non-negotiable there. Claude leans on Brave's independent index. A site indexed well by Bing can be nearly invisible to Claude. Anyone who says "we use the same approach for every engine" has not done multi-engine work.

They fix the foundation before touching content. AI bot access in robots.txt, entity schema, Bing and Brave indexation, and render speed come first. If GPTBot and ClaudeBot cannot crawl you, more blog posts change nothing.

They measure citations and pipeline, not just rankings. A ranking is a visibility number. Being named in AI answers for questions no buyer asks is a vanity metric with a fresh coat of paint. The signal that matters is being the cited answer for the handful of questions people ask right before they buy, and the leads that follow.

I go deeper on the exact [questions that filter fast](/insights/what-to-ask-before-hiring-aeo-strategist) in a separate piece, but those four are the quick ones. A candidate who clears all four is doing the real work.

## Red flags that mean you are hiring a relabeled agency

Some tells are loud once you know to listen for them.

- A guarantee of a specific ranking or citation by a specific date. Nobody controls the engines. Anyone promising it is either naive or lying.
- Reporting built on domain authority scores and traffic charts, with pipeline nowhere in the deck.
- A twelve to twenty-four month lock-in demanded before they have shown a single result. The lock-in protects their revenue, not your outcome.
- Content volume as the headline deliverable, like "twelve posts a month." Volume on a broken foundation is decorating a house with no wiring.
- No live citations for their own brand or any named client. Someone who wins AI visibility should be able to show it, starting with themselves.

Any one of these is a caution. Two of them together, walk.

## What it costs to hire an AI SEO expert in 2026

Pricing is wide because the discipline is young and scope varies. Here are the real 2026 ranges, so you can spot both the overcharge and the too-good-to-be-true.

|---|---|---|

| Consultant retainer | $2,000 to $15,000 per month | Foundation plus ongoing citation growth |
| Agency retainer | $5,000 to $30,000 per month | Volume across many pages and markets |

The number matters less than what sits behind it. A $3,000 audit that ends with a prioritized fix list you could hand to any competent developer is worth more than a $10,000 retainer that produces a monthly traffic chart. Ask what you get in the first 30 days and what measurable signal to expect by day 90. If the answer is vague, the price is beside the point.

## What a real engagement looks like in the first 90 days

Vague scope is where budgets go to die, so make anyone you hire commit to a shape. A real AI SEO engagement is front-loaded, because the foundation gates everything else.

Weeks one and two are diagnosis and unblocking. A full audit of crawl access, indexation, schema, and render speed, then the fixes that need no debate: opening AI bots in robots.txt, submitting to Bing and Brave, and cleaning the entity data that tells a model who you are. This is the least glamorous work and the highest leverage. Skip it and everything downstream is built on sand.

Weeks three to six are architecture and content. Entity schema across your core pages, a small and deliberate set of pages rewritten answer-first so an engine can lift a clean response, and the internal linking that ties them into a topic a model can trust. Not volume. The right pages for the questions your buyers actually ask before they buy.

Weeks seven to twelve are distribution and measurement. Getting mentioned on the sources the engines already cite in your category, tracking which engines name you and for which queries, and tying that back to leads in analytics. By day 90 you should be able to open ChatGPT, ask a real buyer question, and see your name, then see the pipeline that follows it.

If a candidate cannot describe a shape like this on the first call, they are improvising with your money.

## How to tell it is actually working

Set the scoreboard before you start, or you will get shown whichever numbers look best that month.

First citations usually appear in 30 to 90 days for a site with existing authority, and longer on a new domain. Technical fixes land in the first two weeks. What you watch: which engines cite you, for which buyer questions, and whether AI-sourced sessions in GA4 turn into leads. Rankings are a supporting metric now, not the headline. If the report leads with positions and buries pipeline, you hired the wrong person, whatever their title says.

You do not need to commit a retainer to find out where you stand. The cheapest, fastest move is a real audit: what is blocking your citations today, whether AI bots can even reach you, and the two or three fixes that move the needle first. That holds true whether you end up hiring me, someone else, or building the muscle in-house.
